## 1. Strongest workshop concept

### 1.1 What students actually want (hypotheses to test in the first 72 hours)

The value equation: **Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort & sacrifice).**

| Lever | Likely student reality (hypothesis) | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Dream outcome | "A skill that gets me a job, internship, freelance client, or my own business going", plus *confidence I'm not wasting money* | Sell proof-of-skill, not "marketing knowledge". |

### 2.4 Which parts raise perceived value, and why

- **A named, tangible deliverable** raises the dream outcome and likelihood: "You will leave with X" beats "you will learn Y".

- **Beginner templates** cut effort; the "I'm not technical" objection is the biggest drop-off.
- **Published rubric** turns the scholarship from a lottery into a skill test.
- **Skill Snapshot for everyone** converts "I didn't win" into "I learned what to improve".
- **Specific refund promise** (section 3) lowers perceived risk more than any discount.

## 4. Risk-reversal mechanism (the ₹999 Delivery Promise)

### 4.1 What HSIM explicitly promises

If you attend the Sprint, you will:
1. Watch a live campaign build (demo) from start to finish.
2. Complete all **5 steps** of your own Campaign Pack with mentor help available.
3. Receive the **take-home kit** (template pack, prompt playbook, brief bank, career map) within 24 hours.
4. Receive your **Skill Snapshot** within 72 hours of the assessment.
5. Receive the session as advertised (date, venue, faculty, duration) without sales pressure during the build.

### 4.2 Attendance and participation proof (3 touchpoints)

- **Check-in:** QR scan at entry (late entry allowed up to 20 min).
- **Mid-session:** a second QR at the end of hands-on.
- **Exit form:** a 2-minute checklist listing the five promises; they tick *received / not received* and can rate usefulness.
- **Participation = all three touchpoints + a submitted Campaign Pack (photo or upload).**

### 4.3 How eligibility is decided

| Case | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Ticks "not received" on the exit form for item(s) that are reasonably fixable | Fix on the spot or within 24 hours. If still not delivered → refund. |
| Meets 4.2, says a promise was not delivered, evidence shows it was not | **Refund approved.** |
| Meets 4.2, says "I didn't like it" but all promises were delivered | Not refundable under this promise (outcomes and opinions are not the promise), but record the feedback. HSIM *may* offer a goodwill gesture. |
| Did not attend / did not meet 4.2 | Not eligible under the promise (see 4.5 for cancellations). |

**Decision-maker:** a staff member who was *not* on the workshop team, using the check-in logs, uploads and exit form. Decision within 3 working days.

### 4.4 Request and resolution timeline

- **Window:** within **48 hours** after the workshop ends, via a short form or WhatsApp number printed on the exit slip.
- **Decision:** within 3 working days.
- **Payment:** refund to the original payment method within 7 working days of approval (gateway timelines apply). GST handled through a credit note.
- **Why 48 hours and before results are published:** it prevents people from claiming a refund *after* seeing they didn't rank.
- **Scholarship interaction:** a refund request does not affect ranking, but the refund and a discount cannot both be claimed `[CONFIRM with counsel]`. State this plainly in the terms.

### 4.5 Cancellations, rescheduling, emergencies, organiser changes

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Student cancels ≥ 72 h before | Full refund **or** transfer to another session, student's choice. |
| Student cancels < 72 h before | Transfer to another session or the next edition; refund at HSIM's discretion. |
| Emergency (medical, bereavement) with reasonable proof | Transfer, or refund if no session fits. |
| No-show without notice | No refund (disclosed at checkout). |
| HSIM reschedules | Student may keep the new date or take a **full refund**. |
| HSIM cancels | Automatic full refund within 7 working days. |
| Material change (venue far from original, different faculty, shorter session) | Student may opt out with a full refund. |
| Technical/venue failure that prevents delivery | Promise applies; refund or free repeat session. |

### 4.6 Legal review

Before ads go live, a lawyer reviews: refund and cancellation terms, contest rules (is it predominantly skill-based? any gaming/prize-competition issues), advertising claims (Consumer Protection Act 2019, CCPA guidance on misleading ads, coaching-sector guidelines if applicable), GST treatment of ₹999 and refunds, data protection and marketing consent (DPDP Act), minors (guardian consent for under 18), photography/recording consent, and any institutional approvals for the Chandigarh branch. Keep a **claims register**: each public claim, its evidence, who approved it, date.

**Legal-review checkpoint:** Day 4 (before launch) and again after the workshop (before follow-up sales). Terms cannot waive statutory consumer rights; the policy is an *addition* to them.

*Optional stronger version (decide with HSIM):* "Stay the first 60 minutes. If it's not for you, tell the desk and we'll refund ₹999, no questions." Higher trust, higher refund cost. Recommend piloting the standard version first.

## 5. Scholarship competition rules

### 5.1 Structure

- **Fee tiers:** Top 3 → full scholarship on ₹48,000. Next 97 (ranks 4–100) → 20% off (₹38,400 + GST). Next 200 (ranks 101–300) → 10% off (₹43,200 + GST).
- **Make rank meaningful:** if fewer than 300 attend, there are fewer awards than positions *unless* a minimum score is set. Recommend **minimum scores**: ≥ 40/100 for the 10% tier, ≥ 55/100 for the 20% tier. Top 3 need ≥ 70 and must also pass a short viva. `[CONFIRM thresholds]` Otherwise, with 200 attendees, "rank 150" gets a discount for showing up, and the competition feels hollow.
- **Eligibility:** age 17+ (under 18 with guardian consent); present for the full session; valid photo ID; one entry per person; HSIM staff/relatives excluded.
- **Redemption:** award must be used by **[date, real batch start]**, applies to the named course/batch only, not combinable with other offers, non-transferable. Scholarship **seats** are for people who would enrol; unused seats go to the next-ranked eligible person (published rule).

### 5.2 Assessment (100 marks, 40 minutes, beginner-friendly)

| Part | Marks | Format |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Fundamentals** | 30 | 20 MCQs on concepts taught in the session, auto-marked. |
| **B. Applied mini-campaign** | 60 | A new, unseen short brief; submit a 1-page plan. |
| **C. Honest communication** | 10 | Clarity and no misleading claims in the copy. |

**Part B rubric (published before registration closes)**

| Criterion | Marks | What earns full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Audience & goal | 10 | Specific audience, one measurable goal. |
| Offer & message | 15 | Clear promise, relevant to audience, honest. |
| Channel & content plan | 15 | Right channels for budget, realistic 7-day plan. |
| Use of AI with judgement | 10 | Evidence of editing and checking AI output. |
| Measurement plan | 10 | 2–3 metrics, how to read them, what to change. |

**Part C (10 marks)** is a dedicated test of truthfulness. Any invented testimonial or income/job guarantee in the answer loses the marks.

### 5.3 Marking, ranking, ties, verification

- **Anonymised:** answer sheets carry codes, not names.
- **Two markers** score Part B independently; scores differing by more than 8 marks are reviewed by a third.
- **Ranking:** total score. **Tie-breaks in order:** (1) Part B score, (2) score on the questions tagged "hard", (3) a 10-minute playoff task for ties at the top-3 boundary. At the 4/100/101/300 boundaries, all tied participants receive the higher tier (small cost, big fairness).
- **Independent verification:** an **external practitioner or auditor who is not an HSIM employee** (named publicly, with consent) reviews the marking sample and the final ranking, and signs a one-line statement. *If HSIM can't secure someone genuinely independent, don't claim independence;* say "internally verified and spot-checked by [named external reviewer]" instead.
- **Publication:** ranking list by anonymised code and score band, plus the rubric and answer key logic, within 72 hours; each participant gets their own sheet and a **48-hour appeal window** for arithmetic/marking errors.
- **Dates:** assessment on workshop day; results published Day 18.

### 5.4 Preventing misleading career/income claims

- Public **"We never say"** list: job guarantees, salary figures, "earn ₹X per month", "100% placement", unattributed testimonials.
- The scholarship is described as a **fee discount**, not a career outcome.
- Every Reel/ad is reviewed against the claims register before posting.
- Mentors and faculty briefed: the answer to "will I get a job?" is "a portfolio and skills improve your chances; no one can guarantee a job."
- Testimonials only with written consent; mark real ones clearly, with date and context. If there are none yet, show the *work* (campaign packs, rubric) instead.
- A standing disclaimer on the landing page and slides: *"Skills and results vary by effort and market conditions. HSIM does not guarantee jobs or income."*

### 5.5 Keeping non-winners feeling respected

1. **Lead with the workshop, not the prize.** The ads sell the Sprint. The scholarship sits two clicks down.
2. **Be honest about odds.** Say "3 full scholarships and up to 297 discounts across the group", and show the rough odds ("about 1 in 70 for a full scholarship if 200 people attend").
3. **Everyone leaves with something real:** Campaign Pack + Skill Snapshot + completion certificate.
4. **Name what the ₹999 buys.** The scholarship is a bonus on top of a self-sufficient workshop.
5. **Celebrate effort, not just rank:** "Most improved prompt", "Best honest copy", "Best local insight": small, free, real recognitions.
6. **No shame leaderboard:** publish bands for most, names only for winners who consent.
7. **Offer a next step for everyone** (practice briefs, a free resource) so the relationship doesn't end on rank.

## 7. Ethical urgency

| Mechanism | How to do it truthfully |
|---|---|
| **Registration deadline** | Set a real operational cut-off 48–72 h before the event (kit printing, mentor batches, venue headcount). State the reason. Do not extend it. If you ever must, say exactly why. |
| **Attendance capacity** | Publish the true venue and mentor limit (e.g., 120 per session at 1:25 mentors). Show a live remaining-seat count from actual registrations. If capacity isn't reached, don't say "almost full". Keep a real waitlist. |
| **Fixed assessment date** | Assessment happens at the end of the workshop on [date], results on [date]. Not movable for individuals. |
| **Award availability** | State the exact number: 3 full, 97 at 20%, 200 at 10%, with minimum scores and the redemption deadline. |
| **Redemption window** | Tie it to a real batch start date, not an invented countdown. |

**Never:** a looping timer, "only 3 seats left" unless true, invented waitlists, fake sold-out screens, planted testimonials, or "price goes up tomorrow" unless it truly does and is written in the terms.

**What to sell instead of scarcity:** the outcome (campaign pack + skill report), credibility (rubric, faculty), and risk reversal.

## 10. Most important weaknesses to resolve before launch

1. **Demand is assumed, not verified.** Run the Day 1–3 discovery; be prepared to change the concept.
2. **Trust gap for a new Chandigarh branch.** Real alumni, faculty visibility, real work samples, sit-in classes. No fabricated proof, ever.
3. **Contest/legal exposure.** A paid-entry contest with prizes needs a lawyer's review (skill vs. chance, advertising claims, GST, minors, data consent, refund terms). Get this done by Day 4.
4. **The 300-position structure.** If fewer than 300 attend, nearly everyone gets a discount and "ranking" feels meaningless; if all 300 qualify, margin erodes. Use minimum scores, state what happens with unfilled awards, and cap redemption by a real batch date.
5. **Discount cannibalisation.** Some awardees would have enrolled anyway. Track how many awards convert and whether non-winners convert at full price.
6. **Refund gaming and operations.** Needs a named independent decision-maker, a clean evidence trail, and a rule that claims close before results are published.
7. **Delivery risk at scale.** 200 people with phones/laptops: Wi-Fi, AI-tool login/rate limits, mentor ratio. Plan an offline-friendly worksheet and pre-generated fallback outputs. Run a full dry run on Day 13.
8. **Independent verification must be real.** If you can't secure a genuinely external reviewer, don't claim one.
9. **Time compression and festival calendar.** About 12 promotion days; Diwali risk (section 0). Confirm the date first.
10. **Economics depend on admissions, not ₹999.** If conversion is below ~3% at the observed cost per attendee, the campaign loses money. Decide the acceptable loss (if any) up front.
11. **The course must match the promise.** The workshop sets expectations; the ₹48,000 course (trainers, syllabus, support, published refund policy) must meet them. Pressure-selling afterwards would destroy the trust you just built.
12. **Data and consent.** WhatsApp marketing opt-ins, parent contact numbers, recording/photo consent, and secure storage of answer sheets and IDs.
